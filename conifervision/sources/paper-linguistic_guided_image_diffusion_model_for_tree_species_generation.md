# LGINet: Linguistic Guided Image Diffusion Model for Tree Species Generation and Identification

## Summary

This paper introduces **LGINet** (Linguistic Guided Image Diffusion Network), a multimodal framework designed to address the scarcity of labeled training data in aerial tree species identification. The framework consists of three main components:
1. A **Forestry Text Encoder** combining Word2Vec (pre-trained on a 1.2M-word forestry corpus) and a Transformer encoder to generate dense, domain-specific semantic embeddings.
2. A **Text-Guided Diffusion Model** utilizing an improved U-Net architecture with Spatial Transformer cross-attention modules to synthesize realistic $32 \times 32$ pixel tree canopy patches from textual descriptions.
3. An **Enhanced YOLOv11 Detector** incorporating a Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN) and attention-augmented classification branches to detect and classify tree crowns within composite $640 \times 640$ pixel forest scenes.

The authors demonstrate that integrating synthetic images generated by LGINet into the training pipeline significantly improves downstream object detection and species classification performance.

---

## Key claims

- **High-Fidelity Generation:** The text-guided diffusion model achieves a Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) of **0.94**, a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of **6.42**, and an Inception Score (IS) of **9.89** on the forest image dataset, outperforming standard GAN and VAE baselines (p. 1, 10).
- **Improved Detection Performance:** The enhanced YOLOv11 pipeline trained with a 1:1 ratio of real to synthetic images achieves a mean Average Precision ($\text{mAP}_{50}$) of **0.868** across dominant tree species (p. 1, 11).
- **Semantic Controllability:** The use of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) with an optimal text prompt weight of **1.0** balances conditional fidelity and generative diversity, preventing semantic drift (p. 11).
- **Data Augmentation Synergy:** Increasing the synthetic data ratio from 0% to 100% (1:1 ratio) yields notable precision gains, while expanding it to 200% yields diminishing returns (+0.002 precision) (p. 11).

## Implications for our pipeline

- **Synthetic Data Augmentation:** Our current [[methods/dinov3-classification]] pipeline relies on clustering and weak labels to handle limited ground truth. LGINet's approach suggests we could generate high-fidelity synthetic canopy crops of rare species (e.g., using text prompts describing phenological stages or lighting conditions) to balance our training sets.
- **Resolution Constraints:** LGINet generates patches at $32 \times 32$ pixels to balance computational efficiency (p. 3). For our high-resolution drone orthophotos, $32 \times 32$ crops may lack the fine-grained structural details required for DINOv3 feature extraction. Scaling the diffusion model to higher resolutions (e.g., $128 \times 128$ or $256 \times 256$) would be necessary but computationally expensive.
- **Computational Overhead:** The paper notes that LGINet requires **5.2 seconds** and **4.5 GFLOPs** per image generation on an NVIDIA A100 (p. 10, 15). This latency profile makes real-time or on-the-fly generation impractical; synthetic generation must be run as an offline batch process during dataset preparation.

## Replication pseudocode

### Prerequisites

- PyTorch, Diffusers library, and HuggingFace Transformers.
- Pre-trained Word2Vec model trained on domain-specific forestry texts.
- A dataset of paired tree canopy images ($32 \times 32$) and textual descriptions.

### Procedure

```text
1. Text Embedding Generation:
   a. Tokenize input text prompt describing the tree species and phenology.
   b. Map tokens to 512-dimensional vectors using the pre-trained Word2Vec model.
   c. Pass token vectors through an 8-head Transformer encoder to output a 512-d sentence embedding (ESi).

2. Forward Diffusion (Noise Injection):
   a. Define linear noise schedule beta_t from 1e-4 to 0.02 over T=1000 steps.
   b. Inject Gaussian noise into the clean image patch I(x0) to obtain noisy image I(xt).

3. Reverse Diffusion (Denoising with LGINet):
   a. Initialize random Gaussian noise I(xT) ~ N(0, I).
   b. For t = T down to 1:
      i.   Compute timestep embedding using sinusoidal positional encoding.
      ii.  Pass I(xt), ESi, and timestep embedding into the improved U-Net.
      iii. Inject text embeddings into the U-Net via cross-attention in Spatial Transformer modules.
      iv.  Predict noise epsilon_theta.
      v.   Perform one-step denoising to compute I(xt-1) using the reparameterization formula:
           I(xt-1) = (1 / sqrt(1 - beta_t)) * (I(xt) - (beta_t / sqrt(1 - alpha_bar_t)) * epsilon_theta) + sigma_t * epsilon_phi
   c. Output generated image patch I(x0).

4. Downstream Training:
   a. Tile generated 32x32 patches into 640x640 composite scenes.
   b. Train the enhanced YOLOv11 detector using a mix of real and synthetic composite scenes.
```

### Gaps / not specified in paper

- **Tiling Artifacts:** The paper mentions assembling $32 \times 32$ patches into $640 \times 640$ composite images to simulate realistic forest scenes (p. 7), but does not detail how edge discontinuities or blending between adjacent synthetic patches were handled.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning for BiFPN:** The exact configuration, layer count, and learning rates for the BiFPN cross-scale connections in the modified YOLOv11 are TBD.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance Implementation:** The exact mathematical formulation of the joint conditional-unconditional training step for CFG is omitted.
